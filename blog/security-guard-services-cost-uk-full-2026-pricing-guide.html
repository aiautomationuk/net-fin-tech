Security Guard Services Cost UK: Full 2026 Pricing & Guide

26 September 2026

Every UK business owner faces the same unsettling question: how much should you pay to keep your premises safe? With rising incidents of theft, vandalism, and unauthorised access, the true security guard services cost UK becomes a crucial consideration in 2026. As security needs grow, understanding the full range of costs and options is essential for protecting your people and assets.

Quick answer: The typical security guard services cost UK businesses between £13 and £25 per hour per guard in 2026. Costs are higher in London and for specialist services, while longer contracts or lower-risk sites can offer savings. Always compare quotes and service inclusions for best value.

Key Takeaways

  • Security guard services cost UK businesses £13–£25 per hour per guard in 2026, with regional and service-based variation.
  • Location, risk, contract length, and guard experience heavily influence security services pricing UK-wide.
  • London, major cities, and high-risk sites attract the highest security company costs in London and the UK generally.
  • Affordable security solutions UK-wide are possible by tailoring services, comparing providers, and understanding hidden fees.
  • Compliance, insurance, and vetting contribute to total security guard service fees UK businesses pay.

How Much Do Security Guard Services Cost UK Businesses in 2026?

Average Security Guard Rates by Region

In 2026, security guard services cost UK companies an average of £13–£16 per hour for standard static guarding outside major cities. For London, the South East, and other urban hubs, rates rise to £18–£25 per hour, reflecting higher wages, demand, and operational costs. These prices are for SIA-licensed, uniformed guards performing standard roles such as access control, reception, or patrols.

Comparing security services pricing UK by region, the North West and Midlands (including security services in Manchester) typically offer more affordable security solutions UK businesses need, with entry-level rates for long-term contracts starting from £13–£14 per hour. However, in London, shorter contracts or night coverage can easily exceed £22 per hour, and specialist event or VIP security may surpass £30 per hour per guard.

Price Ranges by Service Type

The cost of hiring security guards UK depends not only on location but also on the type of service. Here’s a breakdown of average 2026 rates:

Service Type Hourly Rate (UK, 2026) Common Use
Static Guarding £14–£22 Shops, offices, construction sites
Mobile Patrols £13–£18 Warehouses, business parks
Dog Handlers £18–£30 High-risk or large premises
Crowd/Event Security £16–£25 Concerts, festivals, sports
Keyholding/Alarm Response £12–£16 (per call-out) Out-of-hours response

For cost-effective security guard solutions, some companies offer package deals or reduced hourly rates for contracts exceeding six months. Always request a full price comparison for security services, including all potential extras.

What Factors Affect Security Guard Pricing in the UK?

Experience, Qualifications, and Licensing

The skillset and background of a guard have a substantial impact on security guard services cost UK businesses pay. All guards must hold an SIA licence, but those with additional qualifications—such as first aid, advanced conflict management, or ex-military service—command higher rates. For example, a highly trained dog handler or a close protection operative may add £5–£10 per hour to the standard rate.

Many SMEs seek a balance between affordable security solutions UK-wide and the reassurance that comes from qualified, experienced staff. Consider what level of training your site genuinely needs to avoid overpaying for unnecessary expertise.

Site Risk, Shift Patterns, and Hours Covered

Riskier sites—such as those with high-value stock, late-night operation, or public events—attract higher security guard service fees UK due to increased insurance and vetting costs. Overtime, unsociable hours, or short-notice assignments can increase the hourly rate by 15–30%. Weekend and bank holiday shifts also typically incur premium charges.

Longer contracts (12+ months) or guaranteed minimum hours enable companies to reduce operational overhead, sometimes passing savings on to clients. Flexible, part-time, or mobile solutions offer budget-friendly security options UK businesses value, but may not deliver the same deterrent effect as round-the-clock coverage.

For detailed guidance on legal responsibilities and SIA licensing, refer to the Security Industry Authority (SIA) website.

Security Services Pricing UK: Understanding Quotes and Hidden Fees

What’s Included in a Security Contract?

When comparing providers, scrutinise what’s included in the quoted security guard services cost UK. Some companies offer all-inclusive rates, covering uniforms, equipment, insurance, and supervision. Others may present a lower headline figure but add VAT, travel expenses, or charges for radios, bodycams, or CCTV monitoring on top.

Security company costs in London and other urban centres often reflect higher employer contributions (such as National Insurance and pension), more expensive training, and increased public liability insurance. Always check for hidden costs, including emergency call-out fees, holiday cover, or charges for bespoke reporting.

Comparing Value, Not Just Price

Don’t be lured by the cheapest quote. Assess whether the provider includes rigorous vetting, SIA-compliant training, and robust supervision. Reputable providers in the United Kingdom will supply references, insurance documents, and clear, itemised contracts. This transparency reduces the risk of mid-contract price hikes or unexpected liabilities.

UK Security Guard Salary Rates, Company Overheads, and Market Trends

UK Security Guard Salary Rates in 2026

At the heart of security services pricing UK is the guard’s take-home pay. In 2026, SIA-licensed guards typically earn £11–£15 per hour, with supervisors or dog handlers earning up to £18–£20 per hour. London and the South East offer higher wages, reflecting increased living costs and client expectations. These salary rates directly impact the security guard services cost UK businesses face across the country.

Security companies must also pay National Insurance, pension contributions, holiday pay, and often invest in ongoing training and vetting. All these factors, plus profit margins, shape the final price you pay for security guard service fees UK-wide.

How Security Companies Calculate Your Costs

Calculating the true security guard services cost UK businesses pay involves:

  • Wage and salary rates (varying by region and skill)
  • Business overheads (insurance, uniforms, equipment, administration)
  • Legal compliance (SIA licensing, DBS checks, training)
  • Profit margin (typically 10–20%)

Smaller, local firms may offer more cost-effective security guard solutions and personalised service, while national firms provide broader coverage and backup. Always weigh cost, local knowledge, and flexibility when choosing between providers.

Trends: Demand, Technology, and Service Options

The UK security sector in 2026 shows increased demand for hybrid solutions—combining physical guards with technology such as remote monitoring or AI-driven CCTV. These integrations can reduce the number of guards required, lowering average rates for security guard services and offering budget-friendly security options UK businesses appreciate. However, initial setup costs for technology can be significant, so calculate total ownership costs.

Making Security Services Affordable: Tips for UK Businesses

Budget-Friendly Security Options UK SMEs Use

For smaller businesses or those on tight budgets, creative approaches to lowering security guard services cost UK include:

  • Opting for mobile patrols or shared services with neighbouring businesses
  • Combining guards with monitored alarms or CCTV
  • Negotiating longer contracts for better rates
  • Requesting only peak-hour or weekend cover
  • Reviewing and adjusting services each year

These cost-effective security guard solutions can bring peace of mind without overstretching your budget. Always clarify exactly what’s included and request a detailed price comparison for security services to avoid hidden surprises.

Prioritising Security Needs and Legal Compliance

It’s tempting to cut corners for affordability, but never compromise on legal requirements. The United Kingdom mandates SIA licensing and appropriate insurance for all commercial security work. Ensure your provider is fully compliant, carries adequate public liability insurance, and delivers proper vetting. This protects your business and keeps you on the right side of the law.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do security guard services cost UK businesses for a single shop?

For a single retail unit, expect the security guard services cost UK-wide to range from £14–£20 per hour for SIA-licensed static guards in 2026. London shops may pay £18–£25 per hour. Always ask for itemised quotes and clarify inclusions.

What factors most affect security guard service fees UK-wide?

Key factors include site risk, shift pattern, contract length, guard experience, and geographic region. Urban and high-risk sites, specialist services, and unsociable hours all raise the final bill.

Are there cost-effective security guard solutions for small businesses?

Yes, options such as mobile patrols, shared services with neighbouring businesses, or combining guards with technology can reduce costs. Comparing several providers is vital for finding affordable security solutions UK-wide.

Does the cost of hiring security guards UK include insurance and equipment?

Often, yes—but always confirm. Reputable providers should include insurance, uniforms, and basic equipment in their quote. Additional charges may apply for specialised kit or emergency call-outs.

Is it cheaper to use local or national security companies?

Local companies can offer lower rates and more flexible, personalised service, especially outside London. National firms may provide more robust backup and broader coverage, but often at a premium.
