How to Apply for a Startup Grant

27 November 2026

How to apply for a startup grant comes down to four jobs: find a scheme that is open, check you qualify, gather the documents and send an application that answers the funder’s questions. This guide takes UK founders through each step. Grants are competitive and no application is certain to succeed, so we also cover what to do if the answer is no.

Quick answer: To apply for a startup grant in the UK, search for open schemes, check the eligibility rules, gather the documents the funder asks for (usually a business plan, forecasts and registration details) and submit through the funder’s own portal before the deadline. Match your proposal to the grant’s stated aims and follow the guidance to the letter.

Key Takeaways

  • Start by finding schemes that are open now and reading their eligibility rules before you write anything.

  • A strong application has a clear business plan, a costed budget and the supporting documents the funder lists.

  • Grants are competitive. Good preparation improves your chances, but it never guarantees funding.

  • In England, local support now comes through councils, combined authorities and growth hubs. Local Enterprise Partnerships no longer receive government funding.

  • A Start Up Loan is a loan that must be repaid. It is not a grant.

What Are Startup Grants and Why Apply?

What a Startup Grant Is

A startup grant is money awarded by government, a local authority, a charity or a private organisation to help a new business start or grow. Unlike a loan or equity investment, a grant does not normally have to be repaid, and you do not give up a share of the business. It does come with conditions, and a funder can ask for money back if they are broken.

The Main Benefits of Grant Funding

A grant can help pay for start-up costs such as equipment, product development or training without adding debt. Winning one can also show later investors or lenders that an outside body has looked at your plans. The trade-off is time, because applications need careful planning and paperwork.

Grants Versus Other Startup Funding

Most founders weigh grants against loans, angel investment, crowdfunding and their own savings. Grants let you keep full ownership, but the money is usually tied to a specific project and there is strong competition for it.

The government-backed Start Up Loan is often mistaken for a grant. Gov.uk describes it as an unsecured personal loan of £500 to £25,000, with a fixed interest rate of 7.5% a year, repaid over 1 to 5 years (checked November 2026). You must live in the UK, be 18 or over and have, or plan to start, a UK business that has been trading for less than 5 years.

Where Startup Grants Come From

Grants for new businesses come from national bodies, local government and sector organisations. Innovate UK, for example, runs funding competitions for innovative projects, and each competition has its own scope, rules and dates. Funders generally want to see a clear benefit, such as new jobs, new products or lower carbon emissions.

Schemes open, close and change names regularly, and many older guides still list funds that no longer take applications. Check on the funder’s own website that a scheme is open before you plan around it.

Do not build your budget around grant money until an award is confirmed in writing. Our guide on how to manage cash flow effectively explains how to plan around that.

How to Find Startup Grants in the UK

Start With the Government’s Finder

The best starting point is the Finance and support for your business finder on gov.uk. You can filter it by type of support (including grants and loans), business stage (including not yet trading and start-ups), industry, number of employees and region. It listed 213 schemes when we checked in November 2026.

Look for Local Support

Many grants are local. In England, Local Enterprise Partnerships (LEPs) used to be the usual route, but the government stopped funding them from April 2024 and expects their functions to be delivered by councils and combined authorities, including growth hub support. Your council or combined authority website is now the place to look, and gov.uk points founders to business.gov.uk for local advice and free support.

Check the Eligibility Rules

Every grant sets its own eligibility rules. These can cover the size and age of the business, the sector, where you are based and what the money can be spent on. Some schemes need a company registered at Companies House, while others accept sole traders or social enterprises. Read the rules before you start, because an application that misses one requirement is normally rejected.

Choose the Right Grant

Look at the size of the grant, what it will pay for, the deadline and whether you must provide match funding, which means paying part of the project cost yourself. Some grants only fund equipment, while others cover marketing or staff. Apply where your plans fit the funder’s aims, not where the amount looks largest.

Keep Up With New Schemes

Check the government finder and your local authority’s business pages regularly, because some schemes open for a short time or close once the money is allocated. Other founders, trade associations and university incubators are also good sources of news.

The Application Process for Startup Grants Explained

Step-by-Step: How to Apply for a Startup Grant

The details vary by funder, but most applications follow the same steps:

  1. Shortlist the schemes that are open and fit your business.

  2. Read the guidance for each one, and note the deadline and the documents required.

  3. Write or update your business plan and financial forecasts.

  4. Gather the supporting documents, such as registration details, bank statements and quotes.

  5. Complete the application form, answering each question in the funder’s own terms.

  6. Submit through the funder’s portal before the deadline.

  7. Reply quickly to any follow-up questions.

What Documents Are Needed for a Grant Application?

Funders commonly ask for a business plan covering your aims, market, team and growth plans, financial forecasts for the period they specify, and a breakdown of how the money will be spent. You may also need company registration details, a VAT certificate if you are registered, quotes for the items you want to buy and CVs for key people. Some schemes ask for evidence of match funding or letters of support from partners.

Key Checks Before You Submit

Read the funder’s criteria again before you send anything. Applications are often turned down because a document is missing or one eligibility rule is not met. Check every attachment, and make sure the figures in your forecasts match the ones in your business plan.

How Long the Process Takes

Timescales vary widely between schemes, so look for the funder’s published timetable. Allow plenty of time to gather evidence, especially if you need letters of support or match funding commitments from other people.

If you also plan to bid for contracts, buyers may ask for health and safety pre-qualification. Our piece on SSIP accreditation explains how that works.

How to Write a Strong Grant Proposal

Core Elements of a Good Proposal

Open with a short summary of your business, the problem you solve and what the funding will make possible. Back up your claims with evidence, such as pilot results or market research. Answer every question in the guidance and keep jargon out.

Budget and Financial Detail

Give a clear budget that explains each cost and shows value for money. Many funders want costs split between capital items, such as equipment, and running costs. Attach quotes where you can, and if match funding is required, say where it will come from and whether it is confirmed.

Show Impact and Fit

Tie your proposal to the grant’s stated aims, whether that is job creation, innovation, sustainability or community benefit. Set out measurable outcomes, such as products launched or jobs created, and explain how you will track them.

Common Mistakes in Grant Applications

The most common mistakes are sending a generic business plan, leaving out documents, using unrealistic figures and not answering the question asked. Proofread everything, use any checklist the funder provides and ask someone outside the business to read it before you submit.

Tips for Applying for a Startup Grant

Prepare Early

Read the full guidance first, then map out a timeline and decide who does what if you have a team. Gather documents early and leave time for edits. It helps to talk your proposal through with a mentor or adviser before you send it.

Tailor Every Application

Apply early in the funding window, because some schemes close once the money has been allocated. Refer directly to the fund’s aims and explain how your business meets them. Write plainly, and do not assume the assessor knows your industry.

Follow Up and Learn

If your application is unsuccessful, ask for feedback and use it next time. It is normal to apply more than once before a grant comes through, and some founders never receive one, so keep other funding options open. Keep copies of every document and email.

Where to Get Help

Local business support services, growth hubs and your council’s business team can often help with advice on applications. Check that the scheme rules have not changed since you last looked, because eligibility can be updated between funding rounds.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the first steps in applying for a startup grant?

Find a scheme that is open now, read its eligibility rules and guidance in full, then list the documents it asks for and work back from the deadline. The government’s Finance and support for your business finder on gov.uk is a sensible place to begin.

Can I apply for more than one startup grant at the same time?

Usually yes, as long as each funder’s terms allow it. Tailor every application to the scheme, and tell each funder about other public funding you have applied for or received if they ask.

What documents are needed for a grant application?

Funders commonly ask for a business plan, financial forecasts, registration details, bank statements, quotes for the things you want to buy and details of the people involved. Each scheme sets its own list, so follow its guidance.

Is a Start Up Loan a grant?

No. Gov.uk describes the Start Up Loan as a government-backed, unsecured personal loan of £500 to £25,000, with a fixed interest rate of 7.5% a year, repaid over 1 to 5 years. It has to be paid back, and you need to pass a credit check.

Where can I find official information on startup grants?

Use the Finance and support for your business finder on gov.uk, which you can filter by type of support, business stage, industry and region. Innovate UK lists its open funding competitions on the Innovation Funding Service, and your council or combined authority can tell you about local schemes.
