Business Credit Card Comparison UK

17 November 2026

This business credit card comparison UK guide shows what to weigh up before you apply: interest rates, fees, rewards, credit limits, eligibility and the protections you do or don’t get. The right card can spread costs, simplify expense tracking and reward everyday spending, as long as you clear the balance on time.

Quick answer: A business credit card comparison lets you evaluate and select the most suitable credit cards based on cost, features, rewards, and eligibility. By comparing options from leading UK providers, companies can optimise financial management, gain access to rewards programmes, and understand which protections apply to their type of business.

Key Takeaways

  • Comparing business credit cards helps identify cards that match your company’s spending profile and financial goals.

  • Understanding eligibility criteria is essential—requirements differ for startups, sole traders and limited companies.

  • Key features to compare include interest rates, credit limits, business rewards, and expense management tools.

  • Choosing the right card can bring valuable business credit card rewards, from cashback to travel points.

  • Regulatory protection depends on your business type: borrowing by sole traders and small partnerships can fall under consumer credit rules, while cards for limited companies usually don’t.

Why a Business Credit Card Comparison UK Matters in 2026

Managing Costs and Maximising Value

Comparing business credit cards in 2026 is about more than just finding the lowest interest rate. A well-chosen card can streamline expense management, offer cost savings, and reward daily business spending. For example, a 1% cashback on £50,000 annual company spend could return £500 per year—money that can be reinvested back into your business.

The Landscape of Business Credit Card Options UK

There is a wide range of business credit cards in the UK, catering to everything from startups with limited trading history to larger limited companies looking for premium perks. Some cards prioritise low annual fees for cost-conscious businesses, while others offer enhanced rewards or tailored insurance. The variety can be overwhelming, which is why a methodical comparison ensures you don’t miss valuable features.

Top Features of Business Credit Cards UK

Interest Rates, Credit Limits and Fees

When comparing cards, pay attention to the interest rates providers quote. Rates depend on the provider and your business’s credit profile, so compare the representative APR and check whether there’s an introductory 0% period on purchases. Credit limits are set by the provider based on your turnover and credit history. Assess annual and monthly fees too: some cards are fee-free, while premium cards charge a yearly fee in return for extra perks.

Rewards Programmes and Expense Management

One of the top features of business credit cards is access to rewards programmes, including cashback, points for flights, or discounts on fuel and office supplies. Equally important are expense management features—such as downloadable statements, integration with accounting software, and the ability to issue supplementary cards to employees, all critical for compliance and VAT tracking.

How to Approach a Business Credit Card Comparison UK

Establish Your Company’s Priorities

Begin by listing your company’s priorities. Are you looking to spread the cost of purchases, earn rewards, or simply track spending more effectively? For startups, the best business credit cards for startups often prioritise accessible eligibility and low fees, while established SMEs may focus on higher credit limits and premium features. Knowing your primary needs will narrow your shortlist and make your comparison more effective.

Eligibility Criteria and Application Process

Business Credit Card Eligibility Criteria UK

Eligibility for business credit cards in the United Kingdom varies by provider. Generally, you must be a UK-registered business, have a business bank account, and some providers ask for a minimum trading history. Directors or partners are usually subject to a personal credit check. Some cards are tailored specifically to startups or sole traders, making it easier to qualify if you have limited trading history or a developing credit profile.

Business Credit Card Application Process UK

Applying for a business credit card usually involves providing proof of business registration (company number or UTR), latest accounts or turnover details, and personal ID for directors. Some providers offer instant decisions online, while others may take a few days to manually review your submission. For limited companies, you may need to supply your company’s latest accounts and confirm your registered office address. For more on this, read our article on how to manage cash flow effectively.

UK Regulations for Business Credit Cards and Responsible Usage

Understanding UK Regulations and Compliance

Most UK card providers are authorised by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), but how much protection you get depends on who the card is for. The FCA’s consumer credit rules cover lending of up to £25,000 to sole traders and small partnerships (two or three partners), while lending to limited companies falls outside them, so read the terms carefully. Businesses, in turn, have a responsibility to use cards only for legitimate business expenses and to keep personal and business spending separate. Non-compliance can lead to cancelled cards and a damaged business credit score. For more on this, see Apply for a Startup Grant.

Staying Secure and Avoiding Common Pitfalls

Whatever protection applies, card misuse remains a risk. Set clear controls for supplementary cards, review statements regularly, and train staff on responsible card use. For added peace of mind, some cards include purchase protection or fraud monitoring as standard.

Advantages and Drawbacks: Weighing Up Business Credit Cards

Key Advantages for UK SMEs

The advantages of a business credit card extend well beyond convenience. Benefits include detailed transaction records (helpful for VAT and HMRC compliance), improved cash flow from interest-free periods, and the ability to set individual limits for employees. Rewards and insurance features can further add value, helping to offset annual fees or reduce everyday business costs.

Common Drawbacks and How to Manage Them

Potential downsides include high interest on unpaid balances, annual or supplementary card fees, and the temptation for overspending. It is essential to set spending policies and review usage monthly. Reading reviews and real-world experiences from other UK businesses can provide additional insight into pitfalls to avoid.

Frequently Asked Questions

What makes a good business credit card comparison UK?

A good comparison weighs interest rates, annual fees, rewards, credit limits, and expense management features, while also considering eligibility and regulatory compliance.

Can startups and sole traders qualify for business cards?

Yes. Some business credit cards are designed for new businesses and sole traders, but they may require a personal guarantee or have lower initial limits.

What does a business credit card application involve?

Application involves submitting company registration, recent accounts, personal details for directors, and may include a credit check. Online approvals are common, but some providers assess applications manually.

Are there risks to using business credit cards in the United Kingdom?

Yes, including the risk of high interest on unpaid balances, card misuse by staff, and potential impact on business credit ratings. Protection depends on your business type, so internal controls are vital.

How often should I review my business credit card?

It’s best to review your card annually or whenever your business circumstances or card terms change, to make sure you’re still getting the best value and fit for your needs.
