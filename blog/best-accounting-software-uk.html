Best Accounting Software UK

20 November 2026

The best accounting software UK sole traders, partnerships and limited companies can choose is the one that fits how your business is set up and keeps you on the right side of Making Tax Digital. For most firms that means cloud software HMRC recognises, with bank feeds, invoicing and VAT built in. Xero, QuickBooks, FreeAgent and Sage are the names you will see most often, and each suits a slightly different kind of business.

Quick answer: Choose software that is on HMRC’s list of Making Tax Digital compatible products, connects to your business bank account and matches your business type. FreeAgent suits sole traders and is free with some UK business bank accounts, Xero and QuickBooks suit growing firms with staff, and Sage suits businesses that want a long-established UK provider.

Why the Right Accounting Software Matters More in 2026

Software used to be a nice-to-have for keeping on top of the books. Since April 2022, every VAT-registered business has had to keep digital VAT records and file VAT Returns through compatible software, whatever its turnover.

The bigger change arrived on 6 April 2026. Making Tax Digital for Income Tax now applies to sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income was over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year, and our Making Tax Digital guide explains who is caught and when.

  • From 6 April 2026: qualifying income over £50,000 (based on the 2024 to 2025 tax year).

  • From 6 April 2027: qualifying income over £30,000 (based on the 2025 to 2026 tax year).

  • From 6 April 2028: qualifying income over £20,000 (based on the 2026 to 2027 tax year).

If you are in, you keep digital records, send HMRC quarterly updates of your income and expenses, then file your tax return by 31 January. Good software does most of that for you, so the deadline becomes a few clicks rather than a weekend with a shoebox of receipts.

What the Best Accounting Software in the UK Should Do

Whichever brand you lean towards, check it covers these basics before you look at anything else.

  • HMRC recognition: it should appear in HMRC’s software finder for MTD for VAT, and for MTD for Income Tax if you are a sole trader or landlord.

  • Bank feeds: transactions flow in from your business account each day, so you match rather than type.

  • Invoicing and reminders: branded invoices, online payment links and automatic chasers all help with reducing late payments.

  • Receipt capture: snap a photo on your phone and the expense is logged against the right category.

  • VAT tools: support for the schemes you use, such as standard, flat rate or cash accounting.

  • Payroll, if you employ people: employers must report pay to HMRC on or before each payday through Real Time Information, so built-in or linked payroll saves a lot of admin.

Construction firms should also check for Construction Industry Scheme (CIS) features, as not every plan includes them. If you are close to the £90,000 VAT registration threshold, our VAT registration guide covers what changes when you cross it.

FreeAgent

FreeAgent is built around the self-employed and very small companies, with a clear dashboard that shows your estimated tax bill as you go. If you already bank with NatWest, RBS or Ulster Bank, it is hard to argue with free, and our freelancer tax guide shows the records it helps you keep.

Xero

Xero is popular with accountants and has a large marketplace of linked apps for stock, payments, time tracking and more. The entry Ignite plan suits very low volumes, so most trading businesses end up on Grow or above.

QuickBooks Online

QuickBooks offers a dedicated sole trader plan as well as company plans with payroll, projects and stock tracking. It is a sensible choice if you expect to grow from one person to a small team without changing system.

Sage Accounting

Sage has been part of UK bookkeeping for decades, and many accountants and bookkeepers know it well. Its cloud product covers invoicing, VAT, bank feeds and cash flow, with payroll available alongside.

How to Choose the Right Package for Your Business

Start with your business type, because it changes what you need. A sole trader needs MTD for Income Tax features, while a limited company needs year-end accounts support and, often, payroll.

  1. List your must-haves. Count your monthly invoices and bills, and note whether you need VAT, payroll, CIS or stock.

  2. Check HMRC’s finder. Make sure the product and plan you want are listed for the MTD obligations you have.

  3. Ask your accountant. Many work mainly in one or two packages and may get you a better price.

  4. Use the free trial properly. Connect your bank, raise a real invoice and reconcile a week of transactions before you commit.

  5. Work out the yearly cost. Add up the plan, payroll fees and any paid add-ons, then compare it with what the time saved is worth.

If you are still looking for someone to work with, our guide on choosing an accountant covers qualifications, fees and the questions to ask.

Is Free Software or a Spreadsheet Enough?

HMRC says free MTD products are available for people with simple tax affairs, although they may limit things like the number of transactions. That can work if you have one income stream and few expenses.

You can also keep records in a spreadsheet and use bridging software, which connects your spreadsheet to HMRC and sends your submissions. It saves learning a new system, but you lose bank feeds, automatic matching and live reports, which are often where the real time savings sit.

Getting the Most From Your Accounting Software

Software only helps if the information going in is right. Set aside ten minutes a week to reconcile bank transactions and attach receipts, and month-end becomes a check rather than a chore.

Use the reports too. A weekly look at money owed to you and bills coming up gives you an early warning on managing cash flow, long before the bank balance shows a problem.

Finally, keep your records for as long as the law requires. Sole traders must keep them for at least five years after the 31 January submission deadline for the tax year, and limited companies generally six years from the end of the financial year they relate to. Cloud software keeps them safely in one place, and a regular review of subscriptions you no longer use is an easy way of cutting business overheads.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best accounting software in the UK for sole traders?

For many sole traders FreeAgent is a strong fit, especially if you bank with NatWest, RBS or Ulster Bank, where it is included free. QuickBooks also has a dedicated sole trader plan. Whichever you choose, check it is listed by HMRC for Making Tax Digital for Income Tax.

Do I need accounting software for Making Tax Digital?

If you are VAT registered, yes: you must keep digital VAT records and file through compatible software. Sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 have needed MTD for Income Tax software since 6 April 2026, with the threshold falling to £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028.

How much does accounting software cost in the UK?

On standard prices in November 2026, FreeAgent costs £19 a month for sole traders and £33 for limited companies, and Xero runs from £18 to £70 a month, all excluding VAT. Most providers offer a discount for the first few months, so compare the full price after the offer ends.

Can I keep using spreadsheets?

Yes, if you pair them with bridging software that connects your spreadsheet to HMRC. You will miss out on bank feeds and automatic matching, so many businesses find full accounting software saves more time.

How long do I need to keep my business records?

Sole traders must keep records for at least five years after the 31 January submission deadline for the relevant tax year. Limited companies generally need to keep them for six years from the end of the financial year they relate to.

Before you sign up, use HMRC’s Making Tax Digital software finder to confirm the plan you want covers your obligations.
