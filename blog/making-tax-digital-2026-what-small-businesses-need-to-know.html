Making Tax Digital 2026 What Small Businesses Need to Know

24 October 2026

Tax season is rarely anyone’s favourite time of year, but for sole traders, landlords and business owners in the United Kingdom, 2026 brings a new level of complexity—and opportunity. With Making Tax Digital now reaching Income Tax, the old ways of filing and record-keeping are starting to disappear. If you’re not prepared, you risk unwanted disruption and penalties, but get it right and your business could run smoother than ever.

Quick answer: Making Tax Digital (MTD) means keeping digital records and sending updates to HMRC through compatible software. It has applied to all VAT-registered businesses since April 2022, and from 6 April 2026 it applies to Income Tax for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000, falling to £30,000 in April 2027 and £20,000 in April 2028 (GOV.UK).

Key Takeaways

  • Making Tax Digital 2026: digital record keeping and online submissions are now compulsory for VAT-registered businesses and for higher-earning sole traders and landlords.

  • All VAT-registered businesses must use compatible software, and so must sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 from April 2026.

  • Income Tax users send quarterly digital updates and still submit a tax return at the end of the year.

  • Delays in the digital tax transition UK can lead to HMRC penalties and business disruption.

  • Choosing the right tax software for UK businesses can simplify compliance and unlock business benefits.

Understanding Making Tax Digital 2026: The Essentials

What is Making Tax Digital?

At its core, Making Tax Digital is about the UK government modernising tax administration. It’s a move away from paper and manual entry, requiring businesses to manage taxes online using compatible software. HMRC’s aim is to reduce errors, improve efficiency, and make tax easier for businesses. MTD began with VAT in April 2019, was extended to all VAT-registered businesses in April 2022, and reached Income Tax in April 2026. It does not currently apply to Corporation Tax.

Who is Affected in 2026?

All VAT-registered businesses must already follow MTD for VAT, whatever their turnover. From 6 April 2026, MTD for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords whose qualifying income (gross self-employment and property income) was over £50,000 in the 2024 to 2025 tax year. The threshold falls to £30,000 from April 2027 and £20,000 from April 2028 (GOV.UK). Limited companies are not affected for Corporation Tax. The rules apply across England, Scotland, Wales, and Northern Ireland.

Key Compliance Deadlines

The HMRC timeline is strict. For VAT, all registered businesses have been under MTD since April 2022. For Income Tax Self Assessment (ITSA), MTD applies from April 2026 for qualifying income over £50,000, then over £30,000 from April 2027 and over £20,000 from April 2028. Missing deadlines can result in penalties, so it’s vital to know your obligations and act early.

MTD Requirements for UK Businesses

Digital Record Keeping for UK Businesses

The heart of Making Tax Digital is digital record keeping. Businesses must keep records—such as income and expense transactions—in a digital format, using compatible software that can send updates to HMRC. Spreadsheets can still be used if they are linked to HMRC through bridging software. Keep your records for as long as HMRC requires: for Self Assessment, at least 5 years after the 31 January submission deadline.

Quarterly Submissions and End-of-Period Statements

Under MTD for Income Tax, you send a summary of your income and expenses to HMRC every three months, then submit your tax return by 31 January after the end of the tax year. HMRC’s goal is fewer errors and more up-to-date tax records. If you’re not sure what to include, Fin-Tech UK’s VAT registration guide covers common pitfalls and reporting tips.

How Will MTD Affect My Business?

The impact of Making Tax Digital on SMEs will vary. If you already use cloud accounting or automated systems, the transition should be smooth. Paper-based or spreadsheet-dependent businesses will face more of a learning curve. Costs will include a software subscription (HMRC lists compatible products, and some are free) and potential training time. However, digital systems often save time, reduce tax errors, and help prevent late payments—a benefit detailed in our 2026 late payments guide.

Benefits and Drawbacks: Impact of MTD on UK Businesses

Benefits of Making Tax Digital for Small Firms

While the learning curve can be steep, the benefits of Making Tax Digital for small firms in the United Kingdom can be substantial. Automation reduces human error, improves cash flow visibility, and makes managing multiple income streams easier. Real-time updates help businesses make better decisions, while digital storage means no more lost receipts.

Potential Challenges and Costs

There are hurdles to consider, especially for microbusinesses and those with limited digital skills. Software, time and training can add up. Late submissions build up penalty points, with a £200 penalty once you reach 4 points, although missed quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year don’t attract points (GOV.UK). If you can’t use digital tools, for example because of age, disability or where you live, you can apply to HMRC for an exemption. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to use MTD if my income is just below £30,000?

Not yet. From 6 April 2026, MTD for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 (based on the 2024 to 2025 tax year). The threshold falls to £30,000 from 6 April 2027 and £20,000 from 6 April 2028, so if your qualifying income is just below £30,000 now, check again each year (GOV.UK).

Can I use spreadsheets to comply with MTD?

Spreadsheets are allowed only if you use recognised bridging software to link them directly to HMRC’s systems. Manual entry or paper-based records alone are not compliant with Making Tax Digital.

What are the penalties for late or missing digital tax submissions?

HMRC applies a points-based system: you get a penalty point for each missed submission deadline, and a £200 penalty once you reach 4 points, with a further £200 for each later missed deadline. Missed quarterly updates for the 2026 to 2027 tax year don’t attract penalty points, but you still need to send them before your tax return (GOV.UK). Late payment penalties are separate.

What if I have poor internet access?

HMRC can give an exemption if it isn’t reasonable for you to use digital tools, for example because of where you live, age or disability. Most businesses are expected to comply and should seek local support to improve connectivity where possible.
